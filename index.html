Before any tiling or waterproofing work is booked, turn the conversation into a record that can be read later. A practical brief names the room, its condition, water exposure and whether the job is repair or renovation. The published service areas are bathroom tiling, wet-area waterproofing, floor and wall tiling, shower repairs and regrouting, kitchen and laundry tiling, and outdoor or alfresco tiling. The point is not to sound technical; it is to stop two quotes being compared on different assumptions.   • Name the zone: shower, bathroom, laundry, kitchen, entry, living space, feature wall, outdoor area or alfresco space.
  • State what is visible: cracking, loose tiles, tired grout, earlier repairs, moisture marks, drainage concern or access limits.
  • Mark the job type: maintenance, repair, renovation, or a combined demolition and refit sequence.

Separate the finish from the waterproof layer

Tile and grout are the visible finish, while the membrane and treated junctions carry the waterproofing duty. That split matters when a later question arises, because the answer depends on which layer failed or was never properly described. Ask the written scope to show preparation, inspection items, exclusions, membrane boundaries, penetrations, threshold heights and handover detail. Where screeding or waterproofing is expected, have it listed as its own item rather than folded silently into a single lump sum.

Cure timing should follow the membrane system, film thickness and site conditions, not a blanket rule. If a provider proposes to manage both waterproofing and tiling, ask them to confirm the South Australian authorisation relevant to that work.

Match the brief to the risk

A leaking shower should not be sent straight to a finish-level fix. Moisture can sit behind sealant, plumbing, substrate movement or the membrane system, so diagnosis comes before accepting regrouting as the answer. Regrouting may suit maintenance, yet it is not a universal leak cure. For a renovation, the order changes: confirm substrate, falls, wastes, penetrations and membrane choice before finishes are ordered, then plan tile modules, niches, trims, movement joints, cabinetry lines and adjoining transitions together.

  • Repair path: identify the symptom source before pricing the surface fix.
  • Renovation path: coordinate plumbing, electrical rough-ins, floor falls, cabinetry and finished heights before tile selection dominates.
  • Wet-zone path: keep splashbacks, floors, appliances and plumbing aligned so set-out is not trapped by fixed heights.

Compare written scopes line by line

The stronger comparison is rarely the shortest quote. Read each scope for the actual surface, expected water exposure, preparation, waterproofing or screeding assumptions and finish details. If an item is absent, ask whether it is included, excluded or still subject to inspection. Useful line items include tile format, layout, trims, grout, movement joints, access, waste, demolition, hazardous-material questions and known product details.

Photos and approximate dimensions can follow the first enquiry, but the written assumptions should still be clear. Where demolition is involved, record access, fixtures that may move, suspected moisture and anything that could disturb sequencing. A scope that cannot be questioned is not yet a scope.

Use local conditions as prompts

The reader question is simple: what condition, exposure or access issue changes the order of work here?

For outdoor or alfresco surfaces, add drainage, slip needs and exposure to the list. For interior floors and walls, ask how transitions to adjoining surfaces will be handled and where movement joints are planned.

Common questions

Is regrouting enough for every leaking shower? No. The guidance treats regrouting as possible maintenance, while a leak may involve sealant, plumbing, substrate movement or the membrane system. Diagnosis should come before a finish-level repair is accepted.

What should be written before waterproofing and tiling start? The scope should identify the surface, water exposure, preparation, waterproofing or screeding assumptions, tile format, layout, trims, grout, movement joints, access, waste and exclusions. For wet areas, membrane boundaries, penetrations, threshold heights, cure sequencing and handover should be allowed for.

Can one provider coordinate both waterproofing and tiling? A provider may propose to coordinate both, but the guidance says to ask them to confirm the South Australian authorisation relevant to the work before treating that arrangement as settled.
